Perception Before Supervision: Self-Contained Visual Distillation from Counterfactual Blind Spots

Researchers introduced a new method to improve the performance of large multimodal language models (MLLMs) in visual tasks, called Contrastive Counterfactual Visual Process Distillation (CVPD), which enables dense and autonomous visual distillation without the need for external annotations or tools. This matters because traditional reward-based methods provide only coarse scalar feedback, whereas distillation offers a richer alternative through dense token-level supervision. CVPD identifies visual blind spots where zooming into a region changes and sharpens the model's answer distribution, while removing the same region leaves the full-image behavior largely unchanged. This approach can enhance the ability of MLLMs to understand and process visual information.

Beyond Naturalness: Probing Automated Text-To-Speech Evaluators on Linguistically Grounded Dimensions

A recent study published on arXiv investigated how automated text-to-speech (TTS) evaluation methods reflect human perception, considering that these methods should capture the distinct aspects of speech that listeners actually perceive, such as naturalness, which can be broken down into specific perceptual dimensions, like fluency or intonation. The researchers created a linguistically grounded annotation schema spanning 10 distinct dimensions and used it to construct the first dimension-level meta-evaluation benchmark for TTS, comprising 860 utterances annotated by trained linguist raters. The results show that Mean Opinion Score (MOS) predictors tend to collapse onto acoustic signal quality, while Audio Large Language Models (Audio-LLM) judges exhibit selective, prompt-dependent detection that does not generalize across all dimensions. This matters because it helps us better understand how TTS systems can be improved to meet the needs of listeners.

Learning How the World Evolves: Extrapolative Video World Models via Latent Dynamics Reasoning

Researchers have developed a new video world model that learns the evolution of the world through latent dynamics reasoning, a method that allows capturing the world's laws of motion from just pixels. This matters because leading video diffusion models have focused on fitting pixels without modeling how they transition over time. The model, called Latent Dynamics Reasoning (LDR), works by numerically integrating lower-order dynamics and having the model regress only the third- and higher-order residual that drives the rollout. This enables the model to capture the world's evolution more accurately, by explicitly accounting for the underlying dynamics that govern how the world changes.
