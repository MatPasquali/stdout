QuoteBench: How Matched Scores Can Hide Command-Path Failures

Researchers have published a study titled QuoteBench, which highlights how matched execution scores can hide command-path failures in natural language coding agents, such as large language models. This happens because these agents issue Bash commands through interfaces that can serialize, wrap, and reparse model output, making it difficult to distinguish between command-generation errors and failures introduced after generation. The study uses an exact final-state validation approach on 56 one-shot tasks, derived from 14 incident-derived families, to measure this boundary, demonstrating that replaying the same response through an added parser can lower success rates by 55.4 to 73.2 percentage points.

SCULPT: Subtractive Composition for 3D Part Generation

A group of researchers has proposed SCULPT, a new approach to 3D part generation that allows for the creation of coherent and editable digital assets, which is important for applications such as product design and animation. 3D part generation is a challenge because it requires objects to be created as a whole, while also exposing their structural parts for editing and reuse. SCULPT uses a subtractive composition technique, which means that parts are created by selectively removing material from a 3D object, rather than being synthesized from predefined layouts or tokens. This helps to avoid problems such as gaps, interpenetrations, and material discontinuities.
