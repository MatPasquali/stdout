### [PAC-MAN: Perception-Aware CBF-RL for Whole-Body Safety in Humanoid Dodgeball](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28623v1)
`arXiv`

A significant breakthrough has been made in the field of robotics, with the introduction of PAC-MAN, a control barrier function-based reinforcement learning framework that integrates perception and safety for humanoids in dodgeball games. This matters because it enables humanoid robots to effectively avoid collisions, using only a head-mounted camera to detect the ball. The PAC-MAN framework combines control barrier safety with deployment-realistic onboard sensing, allowing humanoid robots to safely and effectively evade the ball. Additionally, tests have shown that the resulting evasion policy is nearly as effective as a privileged state oracle, which has access to more complete information.

### [AISPA: User-Centric System Prompt Auditing for Large Language Model Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28617v1)
`arXiv`

A recent study published on arXiv introduces AISPA, a framework for auditing system prompts in large language model applications, which aims to increase transparency and accountability in artificial intelligence systems. This matters because system prompts, which are instructions configured by developers to control the behavior of foundation models, are widely used in commercial artificial intelligence products, but are rarely disclosed to the public or regulators. AISPA examines specific parts of a system prompt and evaluates them along eight dimensions that matter to users, making it possible to identify instructions that are either protective or problematic. The study's authors used this framework to review 3,249 instructions from system prompts in 88 commercial artificial intelligence products.

### [KAISEN: Reproducible Subgroup Fairness Auditing for Clinical Risk Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28608v1)
`arXiv`

A team of researchers has introduced KAISEN, a fairness auditing tool for clinical risk models, aimed at ensuring the equity and accuracy of these models across different patient subgroups. This matters because clinical risk models can achieve strong overall performance while producing significantly different error rates in specific patient subgroups. KAISEN consists of five phases, including subgroup stratification, disparity measurement, mechanism diagnostics, post-hoc mitigation, and drift monitoring. The tool was evaluated on a synthetic benchmark of 16 disease tasks, 15 social-determinant axes, and three prespecified intersections, providing insights into its effectiveness in identifying and addressing disparities in clinical risk models.
