### [SocietyBench: Forecasting Counterfactual Social-World Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04009v1)
`arXiv`

Researchers have introduced SocietyBench, a benchmarking tool that assesses the ability of large language models to forecast the evolution of real-world social events, a complementary social skill to their ability to perform specific tasks, such as fixing bugs or operating graphical user interfaces. This matters because until now, the ability of models to understand and predict social events has been barely measured. SocietyBench collects web news and social media posts from five platforms, creating a timeline that separates factual events and a public opinion layer. Forecasting questions are scored on two orthogonal axes: probability calibration, which refers to how well a model's predicted probabilities match the actual probabilities of an event, and temporal accuracy, which refers to how well a model can predict when an event will occur.

### [WorldCup Arena: Prospective, Leakage-Free Evaluation of Frontier LLMs on a Live Tournament](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04008v1)
`arXiv`

A study published on arXiv introduces the WorldCup Arena, a prospective and leakage-free evaluation of large language models (LLMs) during the 2026 FIFA World Cup. This matters because most LLM evaluations are retrospective, meaning the event has already occurred and the answer is available online, which can lead to memorization rather than actual forecasting. The researchers tested six state-of-the-art LLMs before each match in the tournament, asking them to predict the outcomes of all 104 games, as well as the group winners and the overall champion. The evaluation is considered leakage-free because the answers did not exist at the time the questions were asked, allowing for a more accurate assessment of the models' predictive abilities.

### [TurnSight: Turn-Level Hindsight Self-Distillation for Tool-Integrated Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04007v1)
`arXiv`

Researchers introduced TurnSight, a new self-distillation approach for tool-integrated reasoning, which enables large language models (LLMs) to learn to solve complex tasks through iterative tool interactions, overcoming limitations of existing reinforcement learning methods, which often rely on trajectory-level supervision, limiting fine-grained credit assignment in long-horizon scenarios, and instead uses turn-level self-distillation, which derives supervision directly from execution-conditioned hindsight. This is particularly useful in tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) scenarios, where the ability to learn to interact with tools effectively is crucial. The TurnSight approach has the potential to improve LLMs' ability to solve complex tasks. 
Turn-level self-distillation is a technique that allows models to learn from their own experience, without the need for explicit supervision, which is especially useful in situations where supervision is scarce or difficult to obtain.

### [PAST-Bench: Benchmarking the Foundations of Recursive Self-Improvement in Personal Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04003v1)
`arXiv`

Researchers have created PAST-Bench, a benchmarking tool to test the ability of personal artificial intelligence agents to improve over time through recursive self-improvement, which is the ability to turn accumulated experience into more effective future behavior. This matters because personal agents retain preferences, task histories, tool routines, and learned skills across sessions, but it has not been clear whether retained experience actually improves them over time. PAST-Bench is a benchmark that isolates this question, allowing agents to run through ordered sequences of tasks under controlled conditions. The results show whether agents improve on later tasks and whether those improvements follow the intended pathway of saving, retrieving, and updating.
